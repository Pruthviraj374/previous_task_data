## 1. What the task asks

A cluster running on-policy RL jobs was decommissioned. What survives is the raw per-step rollout log each actor process wrote before every policy update — a `rollout.jsonl` per run, documented in `BUFFER.md`. The agent writes `/app/replay.py`, invoked as `python3 /app/replay.py <run_dir> <out_json>`, which reconstructs GAE-lambda advantages and returns per actor.

- **Agent sees:** `BUFFER.md` (schema + GAE recursion shape) and one sample run (`run-a3f9`, single actor, single generation, one uninterrupted episode ending in a true termination). No expected output for anything ships in the final version — see §3 for why.
- **Graded on:** that sample re-run against a pristine copy (anti-tamper only, not correctness), plus **thirteen held-out runs**, each isolating one mechanism, all-or-nothing.
- **Constraint:** stdlib only, no network, no subprocess.

## 2. The crux, and the invariants that keep it alive

Three independent axes, none exercised by the sample:

1. **Boundary semantics.** `terminated` (true terminal state, bootstrap 0, GAE carry resets) vs. `truncated` (recording cut off, bootstrap from the record's own `bootstrap_value`, carry *still* resets) vs. an unflagged end-of-archive cutoff (bootstrap from `bootstrap_value`, nothing to reset). The GAE carry must zero at *every* `terminated`/`truncated` boundary, not just be consulted for the bootstrap value — this is the one mechanism that survived every disclosure round and is what finally produced the accepted pass@5 result.
2. **Supersession, at two scopes.** A reconnecting-but-not-preempted worker can re-emit a step (`revision`, higher wins, either file order). A *preempted* worker restarts at a new `generation` and never resumes the old episode — every record from a lower generation is void, including tail steps with no same-step revision competitor to lose to. Generation must be resolved before revision.
3. **Ordering.** Multi-actor records interleave by write order (must group by `env_id`); one actor's own records aren't guaranteed sorted either (must also sort by `step`).

**Invariant that makes it work:** the shipped sample is single-actor, single-generation, single-episode, in order, with no superseded records — every plausible wrong reading reproduces it byte-for-byte. `tools/calibrate.py` asserts this for nine naive implementations before any Docker cycle; `tools/mutation_matrix.py` re-confirms all nine at reward 0.0 through the real verifier.

## 3. Dead ends — the central, most transferable finding of this file

**Disclosure of a clearly-stated rule reliably becomes correct code for this model, on this kind of task, regardless of how sparsely it's worded.** This was tested three separate times, each with a different mechanism, each with the same result:

- **Round 1:** `BUFFER.md`'s `bootstrap_value` presence rule was originally a literal biconditional ("present exactly when no `step+1` record exists later in the file"), which was *false* on `h05_truncate_mid` — `deep_review` caught the contradiction and one pass@2 trial's `spec_consistency` verdict FAILed on exactly that. Fixed by stating the rule precisely (episode-scoped, not file-scoped). Result: **pass@2 went 2/2 solved.** The pass@2 difficulty suggestion's own diagnosis: *"BUFFER.md spells out every rule the held-out fixtures are meant to test... Nothing is left to inference."*
- **Round 2:** Trimmed `bootstrap_value` down to a bare, non-prescriptive definition and genericized the GAE recursion's parenthetical (dropped "the portion of A\_{t+1} that belongs to the same episode"). Result: **pass@2 went 2/2 solved again.** Second difficulty suggestion: *"the RL convention, however sparsely worded, is evidently something a strong model already knows cold."* Naming the field as literally "the Gymnasium field of the same name" cost nothing extra — the model didn't need the citation to recall the convention; it already had it.
- **Round 3:** Added a second axis (`revision`) with its tie-break direction stated explicitly ("the higher revision is the real one"). Still solved 2/2, ~15 minutes per trial, zero near-misses. Adding a *third* axis (`generation`), also with its tie-break stated explicitly, **still** solved 2/2 — until both `generation` and `revision`'s tie-break sentences were trimmed to the underlying *fact* (a restart doesn't resume the old episode; a re-emission carries a different revision number) with the resolution left as a one-step inference, and the recursion's boundary-reset justification (the SB3/CleanRL sentence) was dropped entirely. **Only then did pass@2 go 0/2 for real** (1 valid failure + 1 timeout, `difficulty_crux: PASS`).

**The generalizable lesson:** for this benchmarked model, "state the fact, let the reader derive the one-step consequence" reliably survives; "state the consequence directly, however briefly" reliably gets read as a checklist and implemented verbatim. This is a sharper formulation than the doc set's general "disclose facts, withhold conventions" advice — the boundary between the two is not verbosity, it's whether the sentence names the *resolution*, not just the *situation*.

**A second dead end, orthogonal to the first:** disclosing a data-hygiene rule (`generation`'s tie-break) is **not interchangeable** with disclosing an RL-domain rule (`terminated`/`truncated` bootstrap), even though both failed the same way. `deep_review` later drew this distinction precisely when it forced `generation`'s rule back into the open: *"generation... 'does not resume the interrupted episode' does not equally strongly imply 'and therefore discard every later record too, including ones with no colliding step to force the question'"* — unlike `revision`, where "re-emit a step already written" naturally implies higher-is-newer (a near-universal versioning convention). Two axes that look structurally similar (both are "supersede an old record") are not equally inferable; the fix for one is not the fix for the other, and treating them as interchangeable cost a cycle.

**A third dead end, discovered too late to avoid but recorded for next time:** once one field's claim ("bootstrap_value is authoritative regardless of what any other record might otherwise suggest") is written broadly enough to resolve one genuine ambiguity, it can silently create a new one by being *more general than the actual implementation*. The fix that held was to state the field's precise presence rule (not just its priority), narrow enough to make the reference implementation and the documented claim provably consistent, and to audit that rule against every fixture programmatically before trusting it.

## 4. What actually worked

1. **Breadth of genuinely disclosed, genuinely real mechanisms**, not cleverness in hiding any one of them. Every individual rule in this task is stated somewhere the agent can read; the difficulty is remembering to apply all of them, in the right order, simultaneously, under an all-or-nothing verifier — the same shape that got `merge-lora-adapters` (nine mechanisms) and `replay-rungear-runs` (ten mechanisms) to 0/5 in this same category.
2. **Ground truth computed independently in the verifier** (`_reference_replay` in `tests/test_outputs.py`), never imported from `solution/` or `tools/`, so the golden answer can't drift against code the agent's own submission might influence.
3. **A programmatic presence-rule audit** (`python3 -c "..."` one-off scripts checking every fixture against the stated invariant post-generation/revision-filtering) — caught a real mismatch (`h11`'s abandoned-generation tail row) before it ever reached a gate, and was reused to verify the final `bootstrap_value` presence rule against all fifteen archives.
4. **Reinforcing a proven-hard mechanism with a new stress *shape*, not a new mechanism, once pass@5 showed exactly which one was catching agents.** After pass@5 first ran at 4/5 solved with the *only* failure attributable to carry-reset, `h13_rapid_boundaries` (six short episodes cycling through every boundary kind) was added specifically to give a bug that only manifests on a *later* reset more chances to surface than any two- or three-episode fixture could. This moved the needle from 1/5 good-valid-fail to 4/5 in the very next `trials` run, and needed no new BUFFER.md disclosure at all — because it wasn't a new mechanism, it was a harder configuration of the one already proven to discriminate.
5. **Stopping shipping any answer at all**, once shipping-but-not-grading the sample's expected output still drew an `Oracle / Answers Readable by the Agent` (E1) finding a second time. `qc_gate` flags the *presence* of an agent-readable answer file regardless of whether the verifier grades it — `merge-lora-adapters`' fix (grade a different, unanswered artifact) didn't fully generalize here because this task has no second "regression" artifact to redirect grading to; removing the file entirely (matching `sweep-replay` §5.1's resolution) was the clean fix.
6. **Treating a `qc_gate` "needs human review" (advisory) item as load-bearing anyway.** The symlink-guard finding (E5) was advisory on one push and became a `tier1` *required* fix the very next push. Lesson recorded in §6.

## 6. Error → what to do, and what not to do

| Symptom | Do | Do **not** |
|---|---|---|
| `pass2` 2/2 solved after stating a convention, however briefly | Check whether the sentence states the *resolution*, not just the *situation*. If it does, trim to the fact and leave the one-step inference; if that's already true, the convention is simply known — add a genuinely different axis instead | ❌ Assume more trimming of the same sentence will eventually work — it was tested three times on the same field and never did until the resolution itself was removed |
| Two "supersession" axes (here: `generation`, `revision`) both fail the same disclosure test | Treat them independently. Ask, for each: does the *stated fact* (not the rule) uniquely imply the resolution via a near-universal real-world convention (versioning: higher = newer), or does it require a further inferential leap (preemption doesn't resume → therefore *every later record*, including non-colliding ones, is void)? Only the first survives trimming | ❌ Trim both the same way because they look structurally similar |
| `qc_gate` E1 "Answers Readable by the Agent" persists after you stop *grading* the shipped-answer file | The finding is about presence, not grading. Remove the file/COPY entirely | ❌ Assume "ungraded but present" resolves it — measured twice, it does not |
| `qc_gate` lists a finding under "needs human review, not auto-passed" | Fix it in the same push as the "must fix" items anyway if it's cheap. It can become a `tier1` *required* fix on the very next push with no warning | ❌ Treat "advisory" as "optional" |
| Narrowing one field's claim to fix an ambiguity | Check whether the new wording is *general enough to be false* — an unbounded "always wins" clause can silently authorize a scenario (e.g. co-occurring with `terminated`) you never intended and never tested | ❌ Assume a clause that resolves the one case you were thinking about is safe everywhere; audit it programmatically against every fixture |
| `trials` (pass@5) blocked as "not hard enough" (e.g. 4/5 solved) with a clean single-root-cause failure analysis | Read which mechanism the *one* failure hit, and reinforce that exact mechanism with a harder *configuration*, not a new mechanism of unknown difficulty — lower risk of a fresh soundness bug, and directly evidence-backed | ❌ Invent a new, untested mechanism under time pressure; every new BUFFER.md field in this task's history cost at least one soundness-bug cycle |
| Tempted to add many small variations of an already-tested mechanism for "breadth" | Recognize the ceiling: under all-or-nothing grading, a fixture testing an already-tested mechanism adds no catching power beyond the union already covered — it only helps if it exercises a genuinely different *failure mode* of that mechanism (here: repeated vs. one-off reset), and reviewers explicitly watch for "kitchen-sink rule-piling" as a negative signal | ❌ Pad with redundant combinations expecting the raw fixture count to matter |

## 7. Bugs introduced along the way

- **A false biconditional** in the very first `bootstrap_value` presence rule (round 1, §3) — written without checking it against `h05_truncate_mid`, which already existed at the time.
- **An unbounded "authoritative regardless of..." clause** (step 12) that fixed one real ambiguity while silently creating a broader one — not caught locally because no fixture happens to construct the specific scenario (interior continuing step with `bootstrap_value` present) that would expose it; `qc_gate`'s own constructed hypothetical caught it, not any local check.
- **An `h11` generation-abandon fixture whose raw (pre-filter) last row would have failed a naive presence-rule audit** — the abandoned generation's own final row deliberately has no `bootstrap_value` (the worker died before it could report one), which is correct once you filter by generation first, but a first-draft audit script that checked the raw file directly flagged a false mismatch. Fixed the *audit script*, not the fixture — the fixture was right.
- **`tools/gen_fixtures.py` originally wrote an `environment/data/expected/` directory as a matter of course** — had to be removed along with its generation code once the E1 finding recurred; check that dev tooling doesn't quietly regenerate something you've deliberately stopped shipping.

## 8. Process rules learned or reconfirmed

- **Read the freshest sticky comment by `updated_at`, not by scrolling position.** Several sticky comments share identifiers across pushes and are edited in place; a comment that looks relevant can be hours stale. Compare `updated_at` against `git rev-parse HEAD`'s push time before trusting any verdict text.
- **A `pass2`/`trials` "too easy" verdict with a clean, single-root-cause failure analysis is a gift — it tells you exactly which mechanism to reinforce.** Every "too easy" verdict in this task's history came with a precise diagnosis (either the pipeline's own difficulty suggestion or the pass@ analyzer's fail-reasons section); none required guessing.
- **`gh api .../comments -q '.[] | [.id, .updated_at, ...] | @tsv'` plus a targeted `gh api .../comments/<id>` fetch is more reliable than `gh pr view --json comments` for finding the current state of a specific sticky**, especially once a PR has 10+ comments across many pushes.
- **Local calibration (`tools/calibrate.py` + `tools/mutation_matrix.py` through the real verifier) never once disagreed with a live pipeline gate on whether the *design* was sound** — every `qc_gate`/`deep_review` block in this task's history was a genuine documentation/disclosure defect, not a design or implementation defect, and local calibration correctly kept confirming oracle=1.0/nop=0.0 throughout. The lesson: local calibration validates that the mechanism *works*; it cannot validate that the mechanism is *disclosed correctly* — that only a live gate (or a very deliberate manual re-read of the agent-visible files) catches.
- **Never push while a check is in flight** — held throughout across sixteen pushes, checked via `gh pr checks` before every commit.
- **The repo-root `README.md`'s "Design history" section, written honestly in real time as each round happened (including the rounds that made things worse), turned out to double as most of this retrospective's raw material.** Keeping it current wasn't just a pre-push gate requirement; it saved re-deriving the sequence of nine content pushes from the PR's comment history after the fact.

## 9. Reusable checklist for the next task in this category

- [ ] Before assuming "state the fact, not the rule" will produce difficulty, check whether the *fact itself* implies the resolution via a convention the model already knows cold (versioning, standard library semantics, well-known API splits). If so, that axis will not survive contact with a frontier model regardless of how it's worded — budget for at least one additional, differently-shaped axis from the start rather than discovering it three rounds in.
- [ ] When adding a second/third axis of the same general shape (e.g. two kinds of "supersession"), test each independently for whether its resolution is a near-universal inference or requires an extra logical step. Don't assume symmetry.
- [ ] Ship no agent-readable answer for anything that is, or even resembles, a graded shape — presence alone is a finding, independent of whether anything grades it.
- [ ] Treat every `qc_gate` finding as required, including ones explicitly marked "needs human review" — a later gate (`tier1`, or a fresh `qc_gate` pass) can promote it without warning.
- [ ] Any clause added to resolve one ambiguity should be audited programmatically against every fixture (not just eyeballed) before it's trusted — an unbounded claim can be true everywhere you checked and false somewhere you didn't construct.
- [ ] When `pass@5` returns a clean single-root-cause "too easy" verdict, reinforce that exact mechanism with a harder configuration before inventing a new one — it's lower-risk and directly evidenced.
- [ ] Budget for roughly a dozen-plus content-and-disclosure cycles on a from-scratch design in a new sub-category with no prior template — this task took sixteen pushes, and the great majority were genuine soundness/disclosure fixes, not wasted motion.

## 10. One-paragraph version for future me

The crux (a real GAE carry-reset bug at episode boundaries, alongside two real data-hygiene supersession rules) was right from early on; getting it *accepted* took sixteen pushes almost entirely because disclosure is a much sharper needle to thread against a frontier model than the doc set's general advice suggests — three separate mechanisms, tested independently, each got solved 2/2 the moment their sentence stated the *resolution* rather than just the *situation*, and each only became genuinely hard once trimmed to the underlying fact with a one-step inference left for the reader. Two axes that look alike (`generation` and `revision`, both "supersede an old record") are not equally inferable, and treating them the same cost a cycle `deep_review` diagnosed precisely. Meanwhile `qc_gate` repeatedly caught real soundness bugs my own trims introduced — an unbounded "authoritative regardless of..." clause silently permitting a scenario I never tested being the sharpest example — and every one of them was fixed by narrowing a claim to what the reference implementation actually does, then auditing that claim programmatically against every fixture, not by arguing with the finding. When `pass@5` finally ran and returned a clean 4/5-solved verdict with a single, precisely diagnosed root cause (forgetting the carry-reset, and only that), the fix that worked was reinforcing that one proven mechanism with a harder *shape* — six rapid episode boundaries instead of two or three — rather than inventing a new mechanism under time pressure, which is the move every earlier round's soundness bug had come from.
